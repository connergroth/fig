# Idea bank — what the interview knows about

This is the `interview` skill's private reference, **not a library that ships**.
Nothing here is installed. Every entry exists so the interview can (a) recognize a
skill hiding inside something the owner already described, and (b) offer concrete
options when the owner draws a blank. Each archetype is distilled from a skill that
proved itself in real daily use — the shapes are battle-tested even though nothing
about any particular owner survives in them.

A skill only gets written when the owner says "yes, I do that." An archetype nobody
claims is never mentioned again.

Each entry is: **what it does** · *the tell* (what the owner says that means take it)
· the question that surfaces it.

---

## Tier 0 — mechanism, ships regardless

These are the agent operating on itself. They aren't life content, so they aren't
interviewed for, and they don't grow with use.

| Skill | Job |
| --- | --- |
| `skill-creator` | Writes new skills. Every skill below is authored by this one, from the owner's answers. |
| `self-edit` | The agent editing its own runtime, for when a fix lives in the code rather than in a skill. Ships because this is a project, not a product — the owner is expected to change it, and the agent is the fastest way to do that. |
| `vault-lint` | Verifies the vault against its own contracts. The proof the memory hasn't rotted. |
| `reflect` | Daily pass: reads the day and writes only durable memory into the vault. |
| `dream` | Weekly pass: consolidates, prunes, runs the linter. The janitor to reflect's scribe. |
| `setup` | Builds vault structure with the owner — folders, pillars, hubs. Stays installed; the vault keeps growing after day one. |
| `interview` | This bank's home. The cold-start conversation: fills the owner files, probes for loops, suggests from this bank. |

Everything past this line is optional and personal.

## Daily rhythm — the agent acting without being asked

The pillar most owners underestimate. Ask about it early, because a proactive beat is
what makes an agent feel like a presence instead of a command line.

- **Morning brief** — one message at wake time: calendar deviations, overnight mail
  worth knowing, open loops, today's one thing. *Tell:* they check three apps before
  they're out of bed. → *"What do you want to know the second you wake up, without
  asking?"*
- **Evening close** — end-of-day check-in that converts whatever's in their head into
  tomorrow's list. *Tell:* they lie awake mentally re-listing tomorrow. → *"Do you end
  the day with stuff still rattling around?"*
- **Wake enforcer** — pings until they prove they're vertical, escalating. *Tell:*
  they snooze and it costs them mornings.
- **Night cutoff** — a wind-down bell at a fixed hour, framed as work-down not
  bedtime. *Tell:* they say late nights wreck the next day.
- **Heartbeat** — a periodic pulse where the agent asks itself "anything worth saying?"
  and almost always stays quiet. The restraint IS the feature. *Tell:* they want
  proactivity but hate notifications.
- **Commute / traffic** — proactive leave-by time on a known recurring drive. *Tell:*
  they have a fixed commute and hate the parking-lot version of it.
- **Weekly review** — a Sunday pass that cross-references what actually got done
  against the goals they're steering toward and says what to shift next week.
  Accountability, not a recap. *Tell:* they set goals and never look back at them.
  → *"Do you ever actually review your week, or does it just roll over?"*

> Interview note: a proactive skill that fires and says nothing useful is worse than
> not having it. For every beat, ask "what would make you glad it interrupted you?" If
> they can't answer, skip it.

## Inbox and people

- **Email triage** — read each new message, classify it, decide whether it's worth a
  ping, file it. *Tell:* inbox anxiety, or "I miss things that matter."
- **Inbox cleanup** — periodically find the senders piling up unread and actually
  unsubscribe through the browser, not just file them. *Tell:* thousands of unread
  promos.
- **Newsletter digest** — weekly gist of the subscriptions worth reading, one page,
  rest ignored. *Tell:* they subscribe to things they never open but won't unsubscribe.
- **People CRM** — nightly sweep of real interactions (mail sent/received, meetings
  that happened) that updates a note per person: last contact, what's true about them
  now. *Tell:* they lose track of who they owe a reply to, or forget context between
  conversations with the same person.
- **Meeting prep** — log a meeting when it's mentioned, research the person ~20 min
  before, deliver a brief with tailored questions, file their notes after. *Tell:*
  they take calls with people they should have googled.

## Work and career

The most legible pillar for a new owner — start here if they're stuck, because the
output is unambiguous.

- **Application pipeline** — read a posting, tailor the resume, build the PDF, drive
  the portal, submit, write the tracker row. The agent applies; the owner doesn't fill
  forms. *Tell:* any job search at all.
- **Sourcing sweep** — a cheap recurring watcher that banks postings matching hard
  filters, feeding the pipeline. *Tell:* they refresh job boards.
- **Writing voice** — the single source of truth for anything written AS the owner
  (email, DMs, cover answers), plus a loop that sharpens it every time they edit a
  draft before sending. *Tell:* they'd never let an agent send something in their name.
  This is the skill that fixes that.
- **Brag doc** — a running, resume-shaped record of wins at a job or lab, so the
  year-end resume pass isn't archaeology. *Tell:* "I can never remember what I did."
- **Codebase skill** — one per repo they work in regularly: architecture, conventions,
  the gotchas. Loaded when touching that repo. *Tell:* they re-explain the same project
  to the agent every time.

## Learning and knowledge

- **Wiki** — a durable, interlinked set of pages on whatever domain compounds for them.
  The STOCK, as opposed to news FLOW. *Tell:* they research the same topic repeatedly.
- **Coursework / class engine** — pull the LMS state, synthesize readings into
  quiz-ready notes, roll due dates into one tracker, put exams on the calendar. *Tell:*
  they're a student.
- **Deep research** — one question, parallel sub-agents, adversarial verification,
  cited synthesis. *Tell:* they ask questions that deserve more than a search.
- **Explainer video** — plan and render a visual explanation of a concept they're
  trying to learn. *Tell:* they say they're a visual learner and mean it.

## Body and habits

- **Food / macros** — text what they ate, get it logged against targets and told what's
  left. Works only if replying is easier than opening an app. *Tell:* they've abandoned
  a tracking app.
- **Health data ingest** — a phone export (sleep, steps, HR) lands as a file and gets
  parsed, logged, and read back in plain language. *Tell:* they wear a watch and never
  look at the data.
- **Experiment board** — habits on trial with an end date, adherence rolled up from data
  already collected, one adjustment per review, findings promoted when they hold.
  *Tell:* they try things and never conclude anything.
- **Goals** — turn a fuzzy want into a verifiable destination they can be held to. The
  formalizer, distinct from a habit trial. *Tell:* "I've been meaning to…"

## Money and logistics

- **Errand runner** — go DO a transaction inside a site they're already logged into:
  order, reorder, return, redeem a credit, fill an account form. Per-site mechanics live
  in reference files, never in the skill body. *Tell:* recurring purchases, or unclaimed
  perks.
- **Finance picture** — one canonical note for accounts, cards, points, who owes whom;
  everything else links to it. *Tell:* they enjoy optimizing this, or they're bad at it
  and know it.
- **Travel** — plan a trip end to end, price-shopped, points math done, staged for them
  to book. Never books. *Tell:* they travel more than twice a year.

## Capture — the inputs that would otherwise evaporate

Almost every owner needs at least one of these, and almost none name it unprompted.
Ask directly.

- **Save** — a link, video, or article gets consumed for real, then its durable value is
  extracted INTO the note that already owns that topic. Never a fresh orphan file per
  item. *Tell:* a read-later app they never reopen.
- **Stash** — a curated database of sites, tools, and products worth coming back to,
  distinct from ideas. *Tell:* they screenshot things they find.
- **Braindump** — they unload a loop verbatim; the agent captures raw and does NOT try
  to solve it. The writing-it-out is the mechanism. *Tell:* they spiral, or think by
  talking.
- **Bookmarks pipeline** — turn saved posts/threads into real breakdowns, pre-organized,
  instead of a graveyard. *Tell:* a bookmarks folder in the thousands.
- **News flow** — a daily scan of a landscape they must stay current in, delivering only
  what's actually new to them. *Tell:* they doomscroll one specific feed out of FOMO.

## Thinking with the agent

- **Council** — several advisors with distinct lenses take independent positions,
  cross-examine, and a chair delivers a verdict. For genuinely contested decisions only.
- **Stress test** — one question at a time until every branch of a plan is resolved, with
  the agent's recommended answer attached to each.
- **Interview doc** — the inverse: the agent proposes nothing and the owner's own answers
  fill the file, one question at a time. For anything that must be in their words.
- **Ideate** — generate non-obvious candidates from their unfair edges, then kill the
  taken ones cheaply before they get attached.

## Output and creative

- **Page renderer** — when an answer is too long or too structured for a message, render
  it as a real page and send a link instead. *Tell:* they hate walls of text on a phone.
- **Media lanes** — carousels, short video, motion graphics; whatever they actually ship.
  Only worth writing if they post regularly. *Tell:* they're already producing content
  by hand.
- **Playlist / taste** — build sets grounded in their real listening history.

## Agent-as-project

- **Watch** — a passive loop that re-checks a condition on its own cadence and stays
  silent until it trips, then pings once and dies.
- **Grind** — the active twin: a background work-loop that keeps making real progress
  toward an objective across passes until an explicit finish line is met.

## Shape of a good skill (apply to everything written)

1. **The description is the router.** It carries the trigger phrases and the explicit
   NOTs. If two skills could plausibly claim the same request, each says which one wins.
2. **Mechanism in the body, specifics in references.** A per-site or per-repo detail
   belongs in `references/`, never in the skill body or the description.
3. **One job.** A skill that does two things is two skills that disagree with each other
   in six weeks.
4. **Write the failure mode down.** The thing it must NOT do (solve during a braindump,
   book the trip, send the email) is the line that keeps it usable.
5. **A skill earns its place by being used.** If it hasn't fired in a month, delete it —
   an unused skill is bloat by definition, and deletion is free because the vault is git.
